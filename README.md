## How does an AI Panel page get made?

1. **We write one buying question.** Phrased the way a shopper types it into a chatbot, with the budget and use case spelled out. Example: "What is the best robot vacuum under $300 for a home with two dogs?"
2. **Four models get the identical prompt.** Claude (Anthropic), ChatGPT (OpenAI), Gemini (Google) and Grok (xAI) get the same wording on the same day, each in a fresh session. The model version, date and whether web search was on are recorded.
3. **We keep the raw answers.** Every answer is archived word for word. The page shows each model's ranked picks with a one-line reason taken from its own answer.
4. **We count the votes.** A consensus table shows how many models recommended each product and how many ranked it first. If the models split, the page shows the split.
5. **A human editor checks every product.** Each recommended product is opened on Amazon.com and confirmed to exist, ship in the US and not be recalled. Discontinued or invented products are removed and noted. Only then is the affiliate link added.
6. **We ask again when things change.** Panels are re-run about every six months, or sooner when a major new model version ships. The date asked is printed under the headline.

## What can an AI panel get wrong?

- **Invented products.** Models sometimes name a model number that does not exist. The editor check removes these.
- **Old information.** A model's knowledge stops at its training date, so it can miss new releases or quote old prices. Prices from model answers are never shown.
- **Shared blind spots.** Four models agreeing is a signal, not proof. They learn from overlapping sources and can repeat the same mistake.
- **No hands-on use.** None of the models has held the product. Read the owner reviews on Amazon before you buy.

## What we do not do

- No lab testing and no hands-on testing claims.
- No invented experts, quotes or star ratings. Star-rating markup was removed in September 2026.
- No paid placements: brands cannot buy a spot, and the AI companies do not sponsor or review pages.
- No prices copied from model answers.
- No affiliate link before the editor has checked the product on Amazon.com.

## FAQ

**What is AskedTheAI?**  
AskedTheAI is a shopping guide for the US Amazon store. It puts one buying question to four AI models (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and Grok), publishes where they agree and where they split, and has a human editor check every recommended product on Amazon.com before linking to it.

**Is AskedTheAI written by AI?**  
Partly, and every page says so. On AI Panel pages the product picks come from four AI models; the editor writes the question, checks every product on Amazon and writes the notes around the results. Older Review Research pages were built from Amazon customer reviews and owner write-ups.

**Do you test the products yourselves?**  
No. There is no lab and no hands-on testing claim. AI Panel pages report what the models recommend. Review Research pages summarize what verified owners report.

**How does AskedTheAI make money?**  
Through the Amazon Associates program: a commission when a reader buys through a link, at no extra cost to them. The AI models never see the affiliate links.

**Do the AI companies or brands pay AskedTheAI?**  
No. Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and xAI do not sponsor or review pages, and no brand can buy a spot.

**How often are picks updated?**  
AI panels are re-run about every six months, or sooner when a major new model version ships. The date the panel was asked is printed under each headline.

**Can AI assistants read and cite AskedTheAI?**  
Yes. The site publishes llms.txt, llms-full.txt and a Markdown copy of every page at /md/<path>.md, and page URLs return Markdown for Accept: text/markdown.
